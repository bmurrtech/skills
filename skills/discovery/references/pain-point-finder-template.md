# Pain-point finder

Use on an **initial** meeting before a list of pains exists. Lead with pain.
Ask all three questions. Do not add questions unless a capture line is too thin
to map a use case or book a follow-up. Do not solve.

Kickoff, say this in substance:

> We are not designing a solution today. We are looking for recurring work
> where an improvement could produce something the organization can measure.
> We will leave with a few pains, not a backlog.

Always ask:

1. Where is the pain? What recurring work causes the most wasted time, delay, errors, or frustration?
2. What would make it valuable? If that work got substantially better, what measurable change would make the effort worth it?
3. What is stopping us? What would have to be true before you would put an improvement in production?

Steer toward work that is frequent, slow, manual, repetitive, moving data
between systems, searching/classifying/routing, error-prone, or felt by many
people. If the first answer is a solution ("we need a chatbot"), ask what work
that would change.

Stop on a pain when you can fill pain, a rough scale, a value signal, a
constraint or "none heard yet", and who performs the work. Owner name stays blank.

- **Who performs it:** [role or team, not a person's name]

- **Frequency:** [how often, or unknown]
- **Effort:** [rough time or volume, or unknown]
- **Who feels it:** [team, customers, partners, or the wider organization]
- **Value signal:** [hours, cycle time, throughput, errors, cost, or service — the strongest one they named]
- **What they would do with the capacity:** [only if they said it]
- **Constraint heard:** [privacy, policy, judgment, access, systems, ownership, adoption, or none yet]
- **Stakeholder:**
- **Evidence:** [clear / thin] — [what is still missing, one line]

### [Next pain, or delete this block]

## Not solved

[Anything they jumped ahead to designing. Park it. Do not expand.]

## Follow-up

[The single next session this implies: map read-back, or a ranking session. One line.]
